# Glitches & Stitches: artist identity

Written by the owner (2026-10-07). This is the source of truth for anything creative: music, artwork, logos, release concepts, track names, branding, site copy and promotion. Preserve this identity and don't turn the project into a generic EDM brand.

> Take broken pieces. Stitch them together. Glitch them until they become something new. Then use that new thing as another piece.

## Core concept

Glitches & Stitches is an electronic/EDM artist project built around turning unfinished musical fragments into finished art through stitching, glitching, reconstruction and iteration. The name is literal to the creative process:

- **Glitches:** mistakes, artifacts, chopped audio, corrupted sounds, unexpected edits, distortion, experimental processing and happy accidents.
- **Stitches:** taking separate musical fragments, ideas, sections, recordings and previous songs and connecting them into something cohesive.

Unfinished songs are raw material, not failures. A half-finished song can contain a great drop, melody, vocal, texture, transition, drum pattern or sound design idea. Instead of throwing those pieces away, they get extracted and used in future music. The final result should feel intentional even when its source material was fragmented.

## The loop

The project is intentionally recursive:

1. Create a song or musical idea.
2. Finish it enough to become part of the Glitches & Stitches catalog.
3. Extract interesting pieces from it.
4. Stitch those pieces into another track.
5. Glitch, manipulate, transform and reinterpret them.
6. Eventually stitch multiple previous tracks together.
7. Keep building on the accumulated musical library.

This makes "remix²" possible: not just remixing a song, but remixing something that was itself built from previous songs. An eventual album could end with a final piece that uses recognizable material from the whole album, turning it into one interconnected musical organism.

## Creative philosophy

Not every song has to be traditionally perfect. If a song or idea is interesting and enjoyable, it can have a place in the catalog. The project should make it comfortable to release music that might otherwise stay unfinished, because:

- Nothing has to be wasted.
- A mediocre section today could become the perfect texture for a different song later.
- A weird sound can become a signature.
- A mistake can become a transition.
- An unfinished track can become source material.

The catalog itself becomes a musical parts library that keeps feeding future releases.

## Sound

Generally EDM/electronic, but not trapped inside one narrow subgenre. Characteristics can include glitching, chopping, repetition, unexpected transitions, audio artifacts, heavy sound design, resampling, fragmentation, reassembly, layering, experimental textures, clever use of mistakes, and melodic or emotional sections contrasted with chaotic ones.

**Experimental does not mean random.** Even when it sounds chaotic, a finished track should feel like it has an underlying design.

## Visual identity

The visual language communicates: fragmentation → corruption → stitching → reconstruction.

Motifs: digital glitches, broken or corrupted graphics, seams, stitch lines, fragmented shapes, reassembled objects, digital artifacts, misaligned layers, pieces being held together, "fixed" corruption, technical or industrial interfaces, unexpected imperfections.

It can be strange and slightly unsettling, but playful and clever rather than purely dark or horror. It should feel like something that was broken, modified and deliberately rebuilt. How the site applies this: [brand](brand.md).

## Personality

Clever, experimental, playful, slightly chaotic, weird in a memorable way, technically minded, creative, self-aware.

Not generic "dark cyberpunk EDM". The concept is original enough that branding should lean into glitch + stitching + reconstruction rather than falling back on generic futuristic imagery.

## Long-term identity

Glitches & Stitches is not just an artist name; it is a method of making music. The catalog should gradually become interconnected: earlier music becomes material for newer music, and newer music reinterprets older music. Eventually listeners may recognise recurring sounds, melodies, samples, transitions or fragments from elsewhere in the catalog, so the history of the project is embedded inside the music itself.
